On present evidence, no. Sensors measure the air; this edition found no study showing that a low-cost sensor network on its own reduced anyone's exposure or improved health. The records show four separate things:

1. **Measurement.** Networks now cover places that official monitors miss, such as every block of Bihar [C058] and ten Indian cities [C197].
2. **Testing the sensors.** Independent tests exist. A Bengaluru facility tests and calibrates sensors [C201]; its 2026 co-location study found that none of six manufacturers met precision targets for nitrogen dioxide and ozone [C202]. Independent sensor challenges have also run in Thailand, India and Ghana [C169].
3. **Public communication.** Projects publish readings openly and run awareness sessions, for example in Kathmandu schools [C185] and Dhaka's informal settlements [C180].
4. **Policy response.** Some projects aim at a named decision, such as Cebu City's work towards an air quality ordinance [C184]. No source read records that such a decision has yet followed.
