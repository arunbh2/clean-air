## Three ways bad air may affect a vendor

1. **Her body.** Vendors in Chennai who had worked longer had lower lung function, and most coughed at work [C152]. A Delhi study of vendors, drivers and sweepers found many with restricted lung function, though the statistics tied it to smoking and age, not to measured pollution [C151].
2. **Her day.** When offices are told to work from home, a vendor cannot. Staying home means no takings [C086].
3. **Her income.** Restrictions under Delhi's emergency plan stop some kinds of work [C038]. No compensation scheme covers vendors, unlike registered construction workers [C044].

## What studies show, and what we still don't know

| What studies show | What we still don't know |
|---|---|
| People who work in traffic breathe far more soot than fixed monitors record [C031][C032] | How much income vendors lose to pollution, after allowing for weather and season (gap G1) |
| Vendors' lung function falls with years of work (Chennai, cross-sectional) [C152] | Whether air alerts lead to safer choices for people who have to earn (gap G19) |
| Work-from-home advice does not reach daily earners [C086] | Which protections reduce exposure without shifting costs onto vendors |

## What can change

- **The vendor:** a fitted respirator reduces exposure if worn properly, but use in Delhi has been low [C085].
- **Employers and city authorities:** vending zones away from the worst traffic, and shift timing. These are not yet tested here.
- **Public systems:** income support when restrictions stop trade. That is being designed for platform workers under the new labour code, but not for vendors [C103].
- **Funders:** measuring vendors' earnings across clean and polluted days, the first study this story needs (gap G1).
