## 1. A priority opportunity

Delhi's construction workers are legally owed compensation when pollution rules stop their work. Most cannot claim it because their registration has lapsed [C039][C041]. Closing that gap needs outreach and monitoring, not new money for transfers [C101].

## 2. What has been funded elsewhere

| Country | What was funded | Instrument | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| India (Delhi) | Compensation for idled workers (P18) | Public welfare cess | Paying, partly [C043] |
| Bangladesh (Dhaka) | Monitoring, vehicle testing, 400 electric buses (P01) | World Bank financing, US$290m | Board-approved 2025; no results yet [C120] |
| Pakistan (Punjab) | Super seeders, e-buses, monitors (P04) | IDA loan, US$300m | Board-approved 2025; a target, not a result [C122] |
| Indonesia (Jakarta) | Health and economic data for city priorities (P11) | Philanthropic grant | Lessons published; amount not public [C132] |

## 3. Trade-offs to weigh

Electric buses and inspection centres are big public investments whose street-level air benefit has not been measured yet [C120]. Data and outreach grants are small and can unlock public money, but claims that they caused larger investments are usually the funder's own account [C143].

## 4. Financing, upkeep and safeguards

Ask who pays operating costs in year three [C129]. Ask whether results are measured independently, and whether workers or smallholders bear new costs.

## 5. What would change the judgement

If registration help does not raise the share of idled workers paid within a month over two seasons, redesign or stop (pathway DP1).
