## What the crisis is

Fine-particle pollution (PM2.5) is linked to an estimated 1.67 million deaths in India in 2019, about one death in six [C016]. Studies using Indian death records find daily deaths rising with PM2.5 even below the national daily limit [C053]. India's annual standard is eight times the World Health Organization's guideline [C004]. The Air Quality Index stops at 500, and readings above 999 µg/m³ are left out of it, so the worst hours are hidden [C008][C010]. About 40% of districts have no government monitor [C033], and an unmonitored place is not a safe place [C035].

## Why the consequences are unequal

People who work in traffic breathe far more than fixed monitors record [C031]. In Delhi, abnormal lung function was found in 86% of sanitation workers and 75% of waste pickers, against 45% of office workers [C082]. Air purifiers and air conditioning are concentrated among the affluent [C046]. Work-from-home advisories mean nothing to people paid by the day [C086].

## How health connects to work, income and care

Illness can cost earnings and add medical bills [C047]. But the best-documented income loss comes from pollution control itself. When Delhi's emergency plan stops construction, the law says idled workers must be paid, yet compensation reaches registered workers far more often than migrants [C039][C041][C044]. When schools close, children without devices lose most, and caregivers lose working time [C054][C055].

## What you can explore

Follow a group, such as street vendors, construction workers or children, into a place, such as Delhi-NCR or Bihar. Trace the health, income and care consequences. Compare the interventions that could help, with their evidence, who would run them and what they would cost to keep going.

## How evidence and uncertainty are handled

Every factual statement links to a claim, and every claim links to its sources, with dates. Ratings use four labels: Strong, Moderate, Limited/mixed, and No adequate evidence found. Confidence that a problem exists is rated separately from confidence that a response works. Gaps are shown as gaps. Nothing on this site is a medical recommendation or a validated investment recommendation.
