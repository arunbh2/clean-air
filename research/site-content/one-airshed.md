---
title: "One airshed, many governments"
summary: "Why one city cannot clean the air on its own, what neighbours can do together, and how far regional cooperation has actually got."
---
<p class="simple-answer">The Indo-Gangetic plain and Himalayan foothills share one airshed of 13 jurisdictions in five countries and nearly one billion people [C227]. On World Bank model estimates, about half of fine particles in these jurisdictions comes from outside local boundaries [C231], so city action is essential but not sufficient.</p>

## Local action and regional action are both needed

City and state measures on dust, waste burning, buses and industry still matter: in Kathmandu Valley, Uttar Pradesh and Dhaka the local share is estimated at about 60% [C231]. In places such as Nepal's Terai, most pollution is estimated to arrive from elsewhere [C231]. Model shares describe flows of pollution; they do not assign blame to any government.

## The shared target, read carefully

Through the Kathmandu Roadmap (2022) and the Thimphu Outcome (2024), the five countries are considering an aspirational regional target of annual average PM2.5 below 35 µg/m³ by 2035, known as "35 by 35" [C228]. It is an interim milestone equal to the WHO's first interim target, not the WHO guideline of 5 µg/m³, and not a binding commitment by every jurisdiction. Some national standards already meet it and others are moving towards it [C229].

## Five functions of regional cooperation

The World Bank proposes five functions around the target [C262]:

| Function | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Data, monitoring and knowledge systems | Harmonised monitoring, aligned emission inventories, shared modelling |
| Engagement and communication | Regular dialogue, joint publications, open data and public participation |
| Institutional coordination across jurisdictions | A steering body and secretariat with clear roles alongside national and local governments |
| Implementation and solutions alignment | Synchronised timelines for measures in industry, transport, energy and agriculture |
| Finance and resource mobilisation | A regional financing window, moving from donor support to member contributions |

## What exists today, and what is proposed

The Science-Policy-Finance Dialogue, led by ICIMOD with the World Bank, is operating as a dialogue [C262]. The Malé Declaration remains voluntary and under-resourced, and no regional financing mechanism exists [C262]. The steering committee, permanent secretariat and financing window are proposals, not institutions already in place. Elsewhere in Asia, ASEAN opened a coordinating centre for transboundary haze in 2023 [C220].
