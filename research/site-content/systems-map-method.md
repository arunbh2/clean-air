## What the map is, and is not

The map is an illustrative, evidence-annotated description of how air pollution, exposure, health, livelihoods and institutions connect. It is not a quantitative model. It does not predict how much any action would change pollution or health, and it does not add up effects along a path.

## Six layers that are kept apart

1. **Where pollution comes from:** activities that emit particles or the gases that form them.
2. **What happens in the air:** weather, particles formed from gases, and pollution carried across borders. Weather can raise concentrations without any change in emissions.
3. **Where people breathe it:** kitchens, classrooms, roadsides and outdoor jobs. An area monitor reading is not a person's exposure.
4. **Health:** effects on lungs, pregnancies, children and deaths. Associations are labelled as associations.
5. **Livelihoods and households:** earnings, spending, schooling and debt. Unmeasured links are labelled as hypotheses.
6. **Institutions, rules and money:** monitoring, emergency rules, courts, welfare funds and costs. These are levers and conditions, not proof of cause.

## Kinds of connection

| Kind | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Causal evidence | A study design that supports cause and effect, or source-apportionment evidence that a source adds to concentrations. |
| Association | Exposure and outcome move together in observational data; not proof of cause. |
| Mechanism | A physical or institutional process documented in the sources, such as weather trapping pollution or compensation depending on registration. |
| Reasoned hypothesis | A plausible link with no measured study in the sources read. Shown with a dotted line, rated "No adequate evidence found" or "Limited/mixed". |

Every connection is rated with Clean Air's four labels, **Strong**, **Moderate**, **Limited/mixed** and **No adequate evidence found**, with the reason in words. The rating says how confident we are that the connection exists in the documented setting. A lever's rating is separate: it says how strong the evidence is that the action improves outcomes, and it comes from the intervention assessment, never from the strength of the problem it targets.

## Rules for including a connection

- Every measured connection cites claims from the claim register, each with sources, a locator and a verification state.
- A connection rated Strong needs at least one claim with a located supporting passage in a fully read source.
- A hypothesis never carries a Strong or Moderate rating.
- A source naming two organisations does not create a link between them. Organisations appear on a lever only through a verified role on a documented activity record.
- Evidence from one place carries its place. Where a link rests on Delhi or one factory, the uncertainty note says so.

## Why loops are interpretations

A loop is a closed sequence of connections that returns to where it started. Each connection in a loop can have its own evidence, but evidence for each arrow is not evidence that the whole cycle operates. None of the loops on the map has been studied end to end, so each is rated **No adequate evidence found** as a whole, with its weakest link and its number of measured links shown. ## Limits

- The map draws mainly on India, with Delhi-NCR as the anchor; links from Bangladesh, Pakistan, Nepal and Southeast Asia are labelled with their place.
- Livelihood and household consequences are the least measured part of the system.
- Some source studies are old, small or cross-sectional; the uncertainty note on each link says so.
- The map reflects evidence published by 22 November 2026.
