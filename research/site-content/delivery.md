## Four kinds of money, four different jobs

1. **Public obligation and public finance:** regulation and enforcement, public health systems, essential services such as waste collection, and the regional coordination mandate [C261] [C262].
2. **Philanthropic catalytic support:** pilots where learning is genuinely needed, independent measurement, protection of workers and residents during transitions, local participation and organisational capacity. The book notes that cost-effectiveness evidence for health-sector measures is missing [C252].
3. **Commercial capital:** productive equipment and services with revenue, such as shared boiler utilities [C247] or creditworthy clean-cooking enterprises.
4. **Blended and concessional instruments:** guarantees, concessional credit and results-based finance where a documented market failure justifies them [C246] [C242].

Market-based instruments can also raise money: the book estimates subsidy reform and pollution pricing could raise revenue equal to 1.3% of regional GDP by 2030 [C251], and the Surat emissions market cut emissions at lower cost in a randomised evaluation [C249]. Trading suits large monitored plants, not small informal enterprises, farms or households [C250].
