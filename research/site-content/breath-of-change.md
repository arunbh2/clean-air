## Five insights in plain language

1. **Air travels.** About half of fine particles in the region's jurisdictions is estimated to come from outside local boundaries [C231].
2. **Household cooking is everywhere.** It is the only source among the top four in every jurisdiction modelled, about a third of exposure on average [C233].
3. **Technology alone has not cleaned the air.** Cleaner stoves, retrofits, fuels and vehicles have not yet translated into significantly cleaner air across the region [C230].
4. **Protection matters while air stays poor.** About 81% of public-school students breathe air above 35 µg/m³ [C257], and health systems have little capacity for pollution peaks [C252].
5. **Delivery is the bottleneck.** The constraint is fragmented institutions and weak coordination more than missing policies [C261].

## Three jobs that solutions do

The book groups its measures into reducing emissions at the source, protecting people while pollution persists, and enabling frameworks that help solutions spread and last [C240]. Clean Air now labels every intervention with one of these jobs on [what works](../what-works/index.html).

## Where the book needs qualification

- **Model shares are not measurements.** The origin and sector shares are GAINS model estimates for 2020-24; some Chapter 2 figures differ from the overview [C232].
- **Two results for one market.** The book reports the Surat emissions market both as 20-30% lower emissions with 10% savings and as 24% with 36% savings [C248]. The primary randomised evaluation reports 20-30% lower emissions and about 11% lower abatement costs [C249].
- **Proposals are not evidence of effect.** Classroom purifiers, masks and clean-air centres appear as recommendations. The book's mask evidence comes from a COVID-19 trial [C255], and Thailand's clean-air centres are described without outcomes [C254], so Clean Air's ratings for these measures are unchanged.
- **Company figures are not evaluations.** The Surat shared-boiler figures come from the operating company [C247].
- **Regional, not universal.** The 33% household-cooking average is not a Delhi figure, and nothing in the book applies to Southeast Asia.
- **35 by 35 is aspirational.** It is an interim target under consideration, not the WHO guideline and not a binding commitment [C228].
