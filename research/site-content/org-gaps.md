This page is about gaps in what we could find. A gap here is not proof that no one is acting. Organisations that publish little in English are harder to document.

## Measurement without a recorded decision

Since 2024 the EPIC Air Quality Fund alone has funded new open monitoring networks in Nepal, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Malaysia and the Philippines [C154] [C173] [C176] [C178] [C179] [C182] [C183] [C184]. Several aim at a named decision, such as Cebu City's first air quality ordinance [C184] or enforcement against landfill burning in Dhaka [C179]. None of the sources read records that a decision has yet followed. The next step for anyone tracking this field is to record what happens after the sensors go up.

## Who pays after the grant

Monitoring networks need maintenance, calibration and staff. An independent test in Bengaluru showed how much readings vary when devices are not calibrated for local conditions [C202]. For almost every project in this directory, no source says who will pay for upkeep once the grant ends.

## Communities and workers

Women in Delhi's unrecognised settlements are trained as air-quality ambassadors [C195], and residents of Dhaka's informal settlements will have their air measured for the first time [C180]. Beyond these, we found few community, worker or residents' organisations with documented air-pollution work:

- no worker or community group outside Lahore in Pakistan with documented air activity;
- few waste-picker or vendor groups beyond Chintan in Delhi;
- named construction unions only in court reporting, so they remain one grouped record.

## Courts and laws that are not yet enforcement

Citizens in Jakarta won a ruling that seven officials were negligent, upheld in 2023 [C208] [C209]. More than 1,700 residents sued over northern Thailand's haze in 2023 [C214]. Thailand's clean-air bill was still before a joint committee in September 2026 [C212]. Bangladesh's courts have ordered mobile courts and kiln closures [C191] [C192]. In none of these cases did we find a source measuring compliance or cleaner air.

## Places we could not document

Lao PDR, Myanmar and Timor-Leste have no verified organisation-level records in this edition. Singapore and Cambodia are thin: in Cambodia only one rice-straw project is listed, and its source could not be read in full. Paediatric associations in Nepal are named only as grant partners [C171], and a UN-supported "State of the Air in India" assessment is known here only from a grant listing [C172]. The full list of leads we reviewed and why each was or was not published is in the candidate log.

## Blocked sources

Some organisations' own sites refused automated access, including Clean Air Asia and the Climate and Clean Air Coalition. We did not bypass these blocks. Their roles are taken from funder, government or press records instead, and the gaps are logged.
