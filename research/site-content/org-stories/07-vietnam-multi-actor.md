## The problem in everyday language

Children in Hanoi's schools breathe city air every day, and in craft villages old furnaces for recycling metal sit next to homes [C216].

## The actors and their different roles

Live & Learn Vietnam runs a green schools programme [C215]. Winrock International led a pollution-reduction project, with VESDI as a partner in craft villages [C216]. USAID funded both [C215] [C216]. Hanoi's environment and education departments supported the schools work [C215].

## What they did

The schools programme, piloted in 2021, reports more than 69 schools in four districts and later all 93 schools in Dong Anh district [C215]. The pollution project worked on open burning, cleaner furnaces at Binh Yen craft village and a roadmap for vehicle emission rules [C216].

## Who paid

USAID; amounts are not given in the sources read.

## What evidence of change exists

School activities and pilot work are documented by the organisations. No air-quality or exposure outcome is reported.

## What remains uncertain

Whether these programmes continued after 2025. We could not confirm current funding, so both are labelled historical.

## What another place could learn

Working through schools and city departments can spread quickly. When one donor pays for most of a field, its exit can stop the work. Programmes need a public owner from the start.
