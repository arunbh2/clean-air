## The problem in everyday language

Dhaka had acceptable air on only 38 days between 2016 and 2021, according to university researchers [C193]. Brick kilns, traffic and burning waste all contribute.

## The actors and their different roles

Environmental lawyers (BELA) and a public-interest group (HRPB) go to court [C191] [C192]. Smart Air Bangladesh and OBHIZATRIK Foundation are new, small monitoring groups [C179] [C180]. BRAC's governance institute is a research partner [C179]. The World Bank approved a public clean-air project [C120].

## What they did

The High Court ordered twice-weekly mobile courts against Dhaka polluters in 2019 [C192] and shut an illegal kiln in Jashore in 2020 [C191]. New monitors will measure landfill burning and air in informal settlements [C179] [C180].

## Who paid

EPIC Air Quality Fund grants for the monitoring groups (amounts not public) [C179] [C180]. A US$290 million World Bank project for government agencies [C120]. Legal work: not documented.

## What evidence of change exists

Court orders and loan approval are documented. Enforcement results and measured air-quality change are not.

## What remains uncertain

Whether court orders are obeyed. How the loan's plans for kilns and transport reach workers who would bear the costs of change.

## What another place could learn

Litigation, open data and public finance can reinforce each other only if someone tracks whether orders and loan targets are met.
