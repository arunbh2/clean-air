## The problem in everyday language

Drivers, vendors and commuters spend long hours beside traffic. Electric vehicles emit no exhaust where they drive, although the power that charges them may be generated elsewhere.

## The actors and their different roles

The Global Environment Facility (GEF) gave the grant. UNIDO is the implementing agency and the Department of Trade and Industry is the government executing agency [C217]. A consortium of Clean Air Asia, De La Salle University, the Electric Vehicle Association of the Philippines and the Urban Electric Mobility Initiative carries out the work [C218]. Separately, Health Care Without Harm Southeast Asia and 350 Pilipinas campaign for updated air quality standards [C219].

## What they did

The project, approved for implementation in July 2022, focuses on electric public utility vehicles, two-wheelers, batteries and electronics [C217] [C218]. In December 2025, more than 500 people walked in Quezon City for clean air [C219].

## Who paid

A GEF grant of US$3,788,990, with US$77,477,969 of co-financing recorded separately [C217].

## What evidence of change exists

Approvals and activities are documented. The GEF classes the project under climate change. No source measures reduced exposure to traffic pollution.

## What remains uncertain

Whether drivers and small operators can afford electric vehicles, and who bears the transition cost. Whether cleaner vehicles reach the routes where exposure is highest.

## What another place could learn

Transport electrification is often counted as clean-air action without measurement. Adding roadside exposure monitoring to such projects would show whether it is.
