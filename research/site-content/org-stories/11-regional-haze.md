## The problem in everyday language

Smoke and soot do not stop at borders. Researchers are studying how black carbon from South Asia's lowlands reaches the Himalaya and the Indian Ocean [C170].

## The actors and their different roles

ASEAN's Coordinating Centre for Transboundary Haze Pollution Control was soft-launched in 2023 [C220]. The Chiang Mai Breath Council brings local groups together on northern Thailand's haze [C213]. Researchers, including the University of Dhaka and the Maldives Climate Observatory, study black carbon carried across the region [C170]. ICIMOD worked with Nepal's brick industry on cleaner kilns [C187] [C188].

## What they did

The ASEAN centre was launched at a summit hosted by Indonesia [C220]. ICIMOD convened kiln owners from four countries to share cleaner designs [C188]. In Chiang Mai, residents went to court over the haze [C214].

## Who paid

ASEAN member governments (not quantified here). Clean Air Fund grants for black-carbon research [C170].

## What evidence of change exists

Institutions and meetings are documented. We found no source measuring reduced transboundary haze due to these efforts. The kiln design's coal saving is a stated potential, not a measured result [C187].

## What remains uncertain

Whether the ASEAN centre is fully operating. How local fire-management agreements hold up when livelihoods depend on burning.

## What another place could learn

Cross-border pollution needs both regional agreements and local work with the people who light the fires and run the kilns.
