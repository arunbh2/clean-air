## The problem in everyday language

Punjab's winter air can be extreme: on days Lahore's air had passed over crop fires, PM2.5 averaged 175 µg/m³ [C190]. Outside a few cities, public measurement is thin.

## The actors and their different roles

The Pakistan Air Quality Initiative (PAQI) is an independent research group publishing open data since 2016 [C189]. The Urban Unit, a Punjab public-sector company, the University of the Punjab and Habib University in Karachi are new grantees [C176] [C177] [C181]. Pakistan's National Emergencies Operation Center and the Sindh Environmental Protection Agency are public partners [C173] [C181]. The World Bank finances the Punjab Clean Air Program separately [C122].

## What they did

PAQI publishes weekly city briefs and analysis, for example that Lahore's PM2.5 was far higher on days its air passed over crop fires [C190]. With the disaster agency it is adding 70 monitors [C173]. The Urban Unit is installing 160 monitors in 10 cities [C176]. Faisalabad and Karachi are getting their first open networks [C177] [C181].

## Who paid

EPIC Air Quality Fund grants (amounts not public) [C173] [C176] [C177] [C181]; World Bank financing for the provincial programme [C122].

## What evidence of change exists

Data and analysis are documented. No source read attributes a policy change or cleaner air to these networks.

## What remains uncertain

Whether open data from an independent group and official data from public bodies will agree, and who reconciles them. Whether the disaster-management framing leads to health warnings that reach outdoor workers.

## What another place could learn

Pairing an independent data group with public bodies can widen coverage fast. Independence and official adoption pull in different directions, and both matter.
