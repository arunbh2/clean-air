## The problem in everyday language

Northern Thailand suffers recurring seasonal haze, serious enough that residents have taken the government to court [C214].

## The actors and their different roles

Thailand Clean Air Network, citizen volunteers and academics, drafted one of seven clean-air laws [C210] [C211]. The Chiang Mai Breath Council, set up in 2019 by health, business and civil-society groups, works on the northern haze [C213].

## What they did

Thailand's cabinet approved seven drafts, including the network's, for parliament in November 2023 [C211]. The House passed the bill 309-0 in October 2025 [C212]. In September 2026 the House rejected the Senate's amendments 414-2, sending the bill to a 20-member joint committee [C212]. In the north, more than 1,700 residents sued national bodies over the haze in 2023 [C214].

## Who paid

Funding for the network's drafting is not documented in the sources read.

## What evidence of change exists

A legislative process is documented. No source read shows the law enacted by 22 November 2026, so there is nothing yet to enforce.

## What remains uncertain

What the final text will say after the Senate's changes, which the House said favoured business. Who will fund provincial clean-air committees if the law passes.

## What another place could learn

Citizen drafting can set the agenda. A bill is not a law, and a law is not enforcement.
