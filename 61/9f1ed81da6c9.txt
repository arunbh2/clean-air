EPIC Awards 39 New Projects to Improve Air Quality Monitoring and Data Access 
The awardees will enhance air quality infrastructure and drive national-level impact on air pollution across 34 countries. The Energy Policy Institute at the University of Chicago’s (EPIC) Clean Air Program is proud to announce 39 new grantees of the EPIC Air Quality Fund. The Fund, now in its second year, supports in-country groups working to turn open air quality data into national policy change in places where pollution is high and public data is scarce. “The people who can fix air pollution where it is most needed, are already there, but they are rarely the ones getting funded.” says Christa Hasenkopf, director of EPIC’s Clean Air Program. “We’ve watched our current awardees move air quality up their national agendas remarkably fast. This new group includes government agencies, researchers, companies, and non-profits in 34 countries, and I am excited to see them do the same in more places.” The 39 new awardees follow a call for applications that  exceeded expectations  with almost 400 submissions from 83 countries—mostly from countries identified as “higher opportunity” for investment, given their pollution levels and limited resources. The projects will be funded over an 18-month period to enhance air quality infrastructure, share the data openly, and drive national-level impact on air pollution. We’re tracking the reach of our AQ Fund awardees and the opportunities for further impact.   Explore The projects come as air pollution is recognized as a significant challenge globally, shortening lives  more than HIV/AIDS and malaria combined —and draining global GDP by 6 percent, or 1.5 times the cost of COVID-19 every year. Yet, less than 0.1 percent of global philanthropy is devoted to the issue. Of the 34 countries where projects will be deployed, more than ninety percent lack open air quality data, and citizens are losing a combined 3.7 billion life years due to particulate pollution. The 39 new projects will work to change that. The projects, 20 percent of which will be led by government entities, plan to launch 140 government-owned air quality monitors. Additionally, several of the projects aim to advocate for the creation of their country’s first national air quality standard, which currently does not exist in 21 of the awardee countries. Support for clean air infrastructure is already showing policy impact. The Fund’s now 70 awardees are using open air quality data to drive policy across 44 countries where citizens stand to gain a combined 4.3 billion life years, if their air were cleaner. Thirteen awardees have already helped catalyze national-level policy impacts in their countries, while increasing the number of Africa’s open data monitors by 80 percent in 18 months. Seven of the awardees  were honored  at the  Africa Clean Air Forum  in July for achieving national-level impact and transforming how their respective nations talk about—and tackle—air pollution. Impact Series: Learn more about the impact our awardees’ are having  The EPIC Air Quality Fund was launched in July 2024 with a $1.5 million gift from Coefficient Giving and has been expanded through support by additional donors. The Fund aims to expand air quality data access globally and reach 1 billion people by 2030 by supporting in-country organizations in installing monitors and providing open data to communities that could benefit the most. Learn more about the new EPIC Air Quality Fund awardees . We thank our funders—Coefficient Giving, Alpha Epsilon, Adam Winkel, The Jascha Hoffman Giving Fund, Joseph and Michelle Verbrugge, Anne Germanacos, John Davidson, and Anonymous Donors—for making this work possible.
